How a Biotech Career Shaped a New Approach to Wellness

📅 August 14, 2026
✎ Craig Levinson
⌚ 6 min read

Most people who build wellness practices come from fitness or health coaching. Craig Levinson came from enterprise biotechnology, and that difference in origin shapes nearly every decision behind Velocity Performance and Wellness.

Over twenty-five years, Levinson held leadership positions at Cytiva (formerly GE Healthcare Life Sciences), Sigma-Aldrich, Waters Corporation, and Packard Bioscience. Those roles put him in direct contact with the technologies shaping modern medicine and research, and required a level of scientific literacy that most wellness founders never need to develop. A fuller account of Craig Levinson's career and philosophy is available on the about page.

An Industry Built on Evidence

Biotechnology is an industry where a claim without data does not survive scrutiny for long. That standard followed Levinson into Velocity Performance and Wellness, informing how the practice selects, integrates, and applies health technologies. Trend-chasing has little place in a model built by someone trained to ask for the evidence first.

It also shapes how Craig thinks about his own clients. Where a wellness-first founder might lead with motivation or inspiration, Levinson leads with a baseline: what does the diagnostic data actually show, and what does that mean for a realistic plan.

He came from an industry built on evidence, measurement, and rigor. That origin shapes how Velocity operates.

From Portfolio Management to Personalized Plans

Levinson's biotech career centered on multimillion-dollar portfolio management and consultative, relationship-driven sales. Those disciplines translate more directly into wellness than they might appear to. Managing a portfolio means tracking many variables against a target; managing a client's performance and recovery is not fundamentally different in structure, only in subject.

That translation is why Velocity Performance and Wellness, detailed further in the story behind the practice's founding, operates less like a typical studio and more like a consultative, appointment-based service built around each client's specific data.

What It Means for Clients in Carmel, Indiana

For clients across Carmel and the greater Indianapolis area, this background translates into a practical difference: recommendations grounded in measurement rather than in whatever approach happens to be trending. Craig Levinson's team applies technology and diagnostics to build a plan specific to the person in front of them.

That same discipline extends to how the practice is run day to day. More on the specific technologies and diagnostics involved is covered in Velocity's performance-technology approach, which walks through how the practice turns data into an actionable plan for busy professionals.

Conclusion

Craig Levinson's path to wellness ran through decades of enterprise biotechnology, not around it. Velocity Performance and Wellness carries that evidence-based mindset into a domain that, in his view, has long needed more of it.
